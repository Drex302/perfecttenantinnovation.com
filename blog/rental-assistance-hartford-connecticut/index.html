The honest picture in 2026

Connecticut's Department of Housing funds rental-support and homelessness-prevention programs through regional partners. Program openings, targeting, and payment limits change, so 211 is the best first screen for current availability.

Greater Hartford Legal Aid handles eviction and housing matters for eligible residents. Connecticut also has a statewide right-to-counsel program for qualifying tenants, accessed through evictionhelpct.org.

Eviction deadlines move fastConnecticut eviction notices and court papers have different deadlines. Contact legal aid or Connecticut's Right to Counsel program immediately; an application for financial help does not extend a court deadline.

What can PTI do for you in this situation?

PTI isn’t emergency cash, and it can’t pay a bill that’s due this week. It works once your landlord joins too, which is free for you. Here’s where it fits.

“I’m behind on rent right now, or I got an eviction notice.”

PTI isn’t the right tool yet. Get emergency help first: call 211 and Greater Hartford Legal Aid at (860) 541-5000, the same day you get court papers. Once things settle, PTI is built to help keep you from ending up here again.

“I got help this month, but I’m worried about next month.”

This is where PTI is designed to fit. If you’re already talking with your landlord about catching up, ask them about PTI. Once they join, eligible rent reporting can help build your payment history, and eligible missions and campaigns can earn rewards.

“I’m waiting on the Section 8 list.”

Use the wait. Invite your current landlord to PTI, and your on-time payments from then on can start building a verified rental record, so you’re a stronger applicant when your name comes up.

“I’m moving and need a fresh start.”

Make PTI part of your new lease. Ask your new landlord about PTI when you sign. From then on, your Stay Grade builds a portable rental reputation that follows you to your next home.

“My landlord won’t make repairs.”

PTI can’t force repairs. Report the problem in writing, keep dated photos, and contact local code enforcement or legal aid.

How to apply: get ready before you call

Most programs ask for the same core documents. Having them ready can save days.

Photo ID for adults in the home
Your lease
Proof of household income
A rent ledger or landlord notice
Any eviction notice or court papers
A recent utility bill or shutoff notice
1
Talk to your landlord early
Explain that you are applying for assistance and ask for any payment-plan agreement in writing.
2
Apply to several programs
Do not wait for one rejection or callback before trying the next resource.
3
Put legal deadlines first
An assistance application does not replace a court response or hearing.
4
Keep a written record
Save applications, messages, names, dates, and confirmation numbers.
