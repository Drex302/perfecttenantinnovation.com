What to Say to Your Landlord About PTI: 3 Scripts That Work

The conversation is simpler than you think. Three complete, word-for-word messages — casual text, professional email, and direct pitch — with a line-by-line breakdown of why each part lands.

By Drexton Andrews  ·  ~6 min read  ·  Updated June 2026

Before you send Casual text Email Direct pitch Timing Follow-up

Most tenants who hear about PTI do one of two things: they sign up and stall on the landlord invite, or they never sign up because that step feels like a barrier. It does not have to be. Your landlord is running a business. They usually care whether rent gets paid, whether you will stay, and whether the property is maintained. PTI speaks to all three — your job is to say that in the right words at the right moment.

Pick the script that fits your relationship. Read the breakdown so you understand the wording. Then send it today. For objection handlers and invite-link steps, see the full landlord bridge guide.

Before you send: three things every message needs to do

Every landlord receiving a PTI invite is quietly running through the same checklist. Your message should answer three unspoken questions:

  1. Is this going to cost me money or save me money? PTI uses membership-style landlord pricing — not a percentage of rent like many property managers. Lead with value; confirm current plans on /join/ before quoting a dollar amount.
  2. What is in it for my tenant? A landlord who understands your motivation is more likely to say yes. Earning toward rent and building credit history where reporting is enabled is a reasonable, dignified ask.
  3. Am I being asked to do a lot of work? Signup is designed to be quick. Say that clearly — five minutes is a fair estimate for many landlords.
The mistake most tenants make

They lead with what PTI does for them and never mention what it does for the landlord. “I want rewards” sounds like a favor. “This gives you maintenance tracking, a more engaged tenant, and flat pricing” sounds like a business proposition. Both can be true — order matters.

📱

Script 1: The casual text

Use when: comfortable, informal relationship — you text about maintenance, they know you by name.

Informal

Text message to [Landlord name]

Hey [Name] — wanted to run something by you. I signed up for PTI, which lets me earn rewards toward my rent through brand social media campaigns where my program is active. It can also report my on-time payments to credit bureaus where that's enabled for my lease — something I'm working on.

For you there are tools too — maintenance tracking, tenant messaging, lease docs in one place. It's a flat monthly subscription, not a cut of my rent like a property management company.

I have a personal invite link that connects us when you sign up. Mind if I send it over? Takes about five minutes on your end.

Why this works — line by line

Line 1

“Wanted to run something by you” signals a conversation, not a demand. Credit motivation explains why you're asking without sounding like a hard sell.

Line 2

Pivots to landlord value — tools, flat fee, contrast with PM percentage — before they have to ask what's in it for them.

Line 3

“Mind if I send it over?” is easier to say yes to than “will you sign up?” The five-minute frame removes the complexity objection early.

✉️

Script 2: The professional email

Use when: formal relationship — you email about maintenance, property manager, or you've never texted.

Formal

Subject: Platform I think would benefit both of us — quick question

Hi [Name],

I hope things are going well. I wanted to bring something to your attention that I think you'd find useful as a landlord.

I recently joined Perfect Tenant Innovation (PTI). As a tenant, I earn rewards toward rent through brand campaigns where my program is active, and optional rent reporting to credit bureaus when that's enabled — which I'm actively working on.

For landlords, PTI offers maintenance request tracking, tenant messaging, lease document storage, and reporting tools in one flat monthly subscription. Unlike a percentage-based property management company, the fee does not scale with rent collected on each unit.

To activate my linked tenant features, I need my landlord subscribed. I have a personal invite link that connects our accounts when you sign up. I'd be happy to send it along or answer any questions.

Thanks for your time,
[Your name]

Why this works — line by line

Subject

“Quick question” signals a small ask. “Benefit both of us” frames mutuality — important for landlords who screen email quickly.

Tenant paragraph

Honest motivation humanizes the ask and positions you as a tenant thinking about your financial future.

Landlord paragraph

Flat fee vs. percentage is the line that gets attention if they already use a PM company.

Close

“Happy to send it along or answer questions” gives a low-pressure response path.

⚡

Script 3: The direct pitch

Use when: you need to activate linked features soon, you prefer directness, or a gentler message did not get a response.

Direct

Text or in-person conversation

I want to be direct with you about something. I signed up for PTI, which lets me earn rewards toward rent through brand social media campaigns where my program is active. Optional rent reporting can help my credit file when that's available, and I have incentives to document and maintain the property.

Most of that activates once you're subscribed too. Your cost is a flat monthly fee — confirm pricing on perfecttenantinnovation.com/join — and you get maintenance tracking, messaging, and tenant tools on your side.

Here's my invite link: [your PTI invite link from your dashboard]

I'd appreciate five minutes to look at it. It helps me stay on top of rent and communication, which I think benefits both of us.

Why this works — line by line

Opening

“I want to be direct” signals respect for their time. Landlords often respond well to clear, reasonable requests from tenants they already trust.

Constraint

Stating that linked features need their subscription is the truth — not pressure. It frames signup as helping you stay stable, which many good landlords want.

The link

Including the invite link removes a follow-up step — one less reason to delay.

Close

Financial stability as a tenant is an honest close, not a guilt trip.

When to send: timing matters as much as wording

☀️

Best: Mid-week, business hours

Tue–Thu, roughly 9am–5pm. Decision-making mode, not personal time.

📬

Best: After a positive interaction

Maintenance resolved, rent paid early, or a genuine compliment exchanged.

🔄

Fine: After lease renewal

Renewal signals stability — a natural moment for tools that support tenancy.

🌙

Avoid: Late night or weekend

10pm Saturday reads as disrespectful of personal time regardless of content.

📅

Avoid: Right before rent is due

Two days before the 1st can look like financial distress. Mid-month is safer.

The post-send sequence: what to do after you hit send

1

Send — then wait at least 48 hours

Do not follow up before two business days. Many landlords who will say yes respond in that window.

2

No response after 5–7 days: one gentle follow-up

Short: “Hey — following up on that PTI link. Happy to answer questions. Here it is again: [link].” One follow-up is enough.

3

“Let me think about it” — give space

Reply: “Of course — here's the link when you're ready: [link].” Then wait about two weeks before raising it again.

4

If they say no

Respect it. You can try once more in 30–60 days if circumstances change, or look for PTI-connected landlords when your lease ends. See what to do if they decline.
