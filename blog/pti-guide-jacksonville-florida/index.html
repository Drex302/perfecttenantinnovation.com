PTI for Jacksonville landlords

🏠 Landlord guide · Jacksonville, FL

Florida’s residential framework under Chapter 83 is widely studied by out-of-state investors: no local rent control (state preemption), relatively short non-payment notice periods when properly executed, and deposit rules landlords must follow precisely. Jacksonville adds no separate municipal rent-control layer beyond state law — but always confirm current statutes and Duval court practice with counsel.

Florida landlord-tenant law in Jacksonville (educational)

Florida Statutes Chapter 83 governs most residential leases. Jacksonville does not add a separate rent-control ordinance. Themes to research with counsel:

  • Security deposits: Holding and disclosure rules; 15-day return if no claim; 30 days with itemized notice if deducting — missing deadlines can forfeit landlord claims.
  • Non-payment: 3-day notice to pay or vacate before filing in Duval Circuit Court when properly served and executed.
  • Entry: Florida requires 12 hours’ notice for non-emergency entry in many residential contexts — verify statute text.
  • Habitability: Landlords must maintain premises per applicable codes; tenants have remedies when conditions affect health and safety.
  • Source of income: Florida has no statewide protection requiring acceptance of Housing Choice Vouchers — federal and local program rules still apply when you participate. Research JAXHA requirements before listing as voucher-friendly.
  • SCRA: Qualifying active-duty orders can allow lease termination with proper notice — plan vacancy accordingly near bases.
