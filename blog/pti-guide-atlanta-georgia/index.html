Atlanta-specific landlord notes

Georgia is generally owner-favorable on rent rules

Georgia does not impose statewide rent control. Rent increases and renewal terms still must follow your lease and applicable notice requirements. Evictions are court-driven — timelines vary by county practice.

Housing Choice Vouchers

Voucher programs and local rules change. If you participate (or are considering it), confirm current payment standards, inspection requirements, and any local protections or advertising rules with qualified counsel and the relevant housing authority.

Small owners

Atlanta has a large 1–10 unit owner segment — especially in neighborhoods with lots of SFR rentals. PTI is aimed at owners who want professional tooling without turning the portfolio over to a full third-party manager.

Georgia basics tenants should know

Habitability

Landlords must keep premises fit and compliant with applicable codes; tenants should notify repair needs in writing where possible.

Deposits

Georgia has detailed rules on deposit handling and return timelines — track move-in photos and deductions carefully.

Entry

Reasonable notice is standard practice; your lease may specify timing. Emergencies are different.

Retaliation

Certain retaliatory acts after protected activity can be unlawful — document facts and get counsel for contested cases.

Eviction

Only courts authorize removal; “self-help” lockouts are not the lawful path.

No rent control

Renewal pricing is mostly contractual + notice — negotiate from documented performance when you can.

Practical tenant tip
Georgia’s statutory tenant toolkit is narrower than some states — documentation is your edge. Use dated maintenance requests (in-app logs help), save receipts, and confirm important agreements in writing.

Georgia landlord-tenant law: orientation (not advice)

Georgia’s residential rules are largely in OCGA Title 44, Chapter 7. Use this as a checklist to discuss with counsel — not a substitute for it.

  • Deposits: rules exist around escrow / bonding and return procedures — compliance is operational, not optional.
  • Eviction: dispossessory practice is formal; follow notice and court steps for your county.
  • Lease end / notice: fixed terms vs. month-to-month differ; read renewal language closely.
  • Self-help: changing locks or utility shutoffs to force move-outs is the wrong playbook.
  • Habitability: code compliance + timely repairs after proper notice.
  • Rent increases: no statewide rent cap — lease and notice still govern.

City of Atlanta codes
Atlanta has local housing enforcement layers beyond state statutes. If you own intown units, build a relationship with how repairs and inspections are documented — your maintenance log should reflect reality.
