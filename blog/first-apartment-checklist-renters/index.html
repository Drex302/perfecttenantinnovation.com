Getting your first apartment right is less about buying stuff and more about documentation, systems, and habits. The decisions you make in the first 72 hours determine how protected you are when something breaks, whether you get your security deposit back, and how smooth rent-paying feels month to month.

This checklist walks you through each stage: what to verify before you sign, how to do a move-in inspection that protects you, how to budget for the true upfront cost, what to buy first, and how PTI can reward on-time rent behavior.

Move-in inspection: the most important step renters skip

The move-in inspection is your best protection against unfair deposit deductions. Your goal is simple: create a time-stamped record of the unit’s condition before you unpack.

Do this before you unpack anything. Walk the entire unit with your phone camera and photograph every room. Then photograph close-ups of any damage. Send a written list of issues to your landlord on day one. Back everything up to cloud storage.

Protecting your security deposit (all year)

  • Report maintenance issues promptly and in writing. Small leaks become big deductions when they aren’t reported.
  • Get permission before repairs or modifications. If you change it without permission, it can become your cost.
  • Hang things the “low-damage” way. Use appropriate hardware and avoid excessive holes.
  • Clean before move-out and document the final condition. Take dated photos from the same angles as move-in.
  • Give notice correctly and on time. Notice mistakes can cost far more than cleaning.

The photo set that saves deposits. Take move-out photos from the same angles as your move-in photos, before you hand back keys. Send a short message to the landlord: “Final condition photos attached for records.” Keep backups.
