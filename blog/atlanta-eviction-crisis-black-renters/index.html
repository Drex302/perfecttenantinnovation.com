The numbers came from Princeton University's Eviction Lab, one of the most credible housing research institutions in the country. And they are not abstract. They are neighborhoods. They are families. They are people who went to work, paid what they could, fell behind by days — and found themselves in a legal system designed to move faster than they could respond.

In 2025, Atlanta's five-county metro area — Fulton, Gwinnett, Cobb, Clayton, and DeKalb — recorded more than 144,000 eviction filings. That number exceeds New York City. It exceeds the entire state of Virginia. It exceeds Pennsylvania. For a metro area of Atlanta's size, the Eviction Lab's own researchers called it "astonishing" and described Atlanta as having "the highest level of housing instability in the nation."

And inside that number, a disparity that demands its own reckoning.

White renters represent 31% of the renter population but account for just 15% of eviction cases. The inverse of the same disparity — confirmed by Eviction Lab data.

The Eviction Lab's analysis also found that roughly half of the five-county region's 2025 eviction cases were filed on units in majority-Black neighborhoods — meaning geography and race are compounding each other. Where you live in Atlanta shapes your eviction risk as much as whether you can pay your rent.

Why Atlanta got here: the three drivers behind the numbers

1
Georgia's 3-day eviction notice — the shortest in the South
Georgia law allows a landlord to file for eviction after just three business days of non-payment. There is no mandatory cure period, no required payment plan conversation, no grace built into the statute. A tenant who misses rent on the 1st can legally face an eviction filing on the 4th. For renters living paycheck to paycheck — a disproportionately common reality for Black households in Atlanta's housing market — three days is not enough time to solve a cash flow problem before it becomes a legal problem.
2
Rent increases that outpaced Black household income growth
Atlanta's rental market grew dramatically between 2020 and 2025, driven by in-migration and the city's expanding tech, healthcare, and entertainment economies. That growth lifted rents in neighborhoods that had historically been affordable — many of them majority-Black — faster than the incomes of the households already living there. When rent rises faster than income, the math eventually breaks. Eviction is what that math looks like when it breaks.
3
The COVID-19 moratorium ended — and the backlog landed all at once
Atlanta's eviction crisis accelerated sharply in 2023, after the city's pandemic-era eviction moratorium was lifted. More than 155,000 cases were filed that year alone — a volume that reflected not just new non-payment situations but years of deferred filings entering the court system simultaneously. The infrastructure — legal aid, housing counseling, emergency assistance — was not designed to absorb that volume. Many renters faced the process alone.

What needs to change — and what you can do while it does

Atlanta's eviction crisis is a policy failure as much as an individual one. Georgia's 3-day notice law is among the most aggressive in the country. The state's prohibition on rent control removes one of the tools other cities have used to moderate market-rate increases. The absence of source-of-income protection means landlords can legally screen out Section 8 voucher holders, concentrating the most financially vulnerable renters in the least regulated corners of the market.

Those policy levers are real and they matter. But policy change moves slowly. The 144,000 filings in 2025 happened while advocates argued for reform. The families displaced by those filings needed tools, not timelines.

What you can do right now — regardless of where you are in your housing journey:

  • If you are in active crisis: Call 211. Contact Atlanta Legal Aid the same day you receive any legal notice. Document everything in writing from the first conversation with your landlord.
  • If you are currently stable but worried: Communicate with your landlord before a payment is late, not after. Start building credit and a rental track record now, before you need it.
  • If you are rebuilding after an eviction: PTI exists for this stage. Every on-time payment from here forward is evidence. Every piece of financial literacy you gain is leverage. Every reward you earn toward rent is a dollar that makes the math work better next month.
