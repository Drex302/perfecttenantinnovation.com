How to read this page
All dollar ranges are rounded planning bands for conversation and rough underwriting—not audited market statistics, not “median” in a statistical sense, and not sourced from a single proprietary dataset. Pull 30–60 days of live listings for your ZIP, compare to HUD FMR where vouchers matter, and reconcile with property condition and utilities before you sign or buy.

Investor view: do not stop at gross rent

Ranking cities by headline rent or a simplified gross cap ignores the items that dominate real returns: insurance, taxes, capex, vacancy, collections, financing, and legal compliance costs. Florida and Gulf-adjacent markets can see insurance dominate; Midwest value markets can see capex dominate.

  • Build a ZIP-level rent comp folder (photos, SF, year built, utilities).
  • When vouchers matter, anchor to HUD FMR / PHA payment standards, not blog tables.
  • Use a full cash-flow model—start with Rental property cash flow calculator and Passive income: the truth for expense honesty.

Why we removed “renter %” and city-by-city cap tables
Those numbers age badly, vary by data vendor, and invite false precision. If you need occupancy or demographic context for underwriting, pull Census / ACS products for your tract and vintage—and reconcile with local PMs.

Toy calculator: GRM and simplified NOI cap rate

This widget is for mental math only. It annualizes rent, applies a vacancy haircut, subtracts one annual opex line you type, and divides by price. Real underwriting needs debt service, reserves, capex schedules, and tax treatment.

GRM & simplified cap (illustrative)

GRM = price ÷ monthly rent. “Cap rate” here = (EGI − opex) ÷ price, where EGI is gross rent × 12 × (1 − vacancy).

Frequently asked questions

What city has the cheapest rent in PTI's markets?

Among the metros PTI commonly discusses, Cleveland and Detroit often show lower nominal asking rents for comparable unit types in many listing samples—but “cheapest” depends on neighborhood, condition, and utilities. Always verify with live comps in the specific submarket you are evaluating.

What city has the highest average rent in PTI's markets?

Atlanta and Charlotte frequently print higher nominal 2-bedroom asking ranges in many public listing aggregates than Birmingham, Memphis, Cleveland, or Detroit—but premiums swing by submarket and building class. Pull a fresh 30–60 day comp set for the ZIPs you actually buy or lease in.

What is a good cap rate in these markets?

There is no universal “good” cap rate—gross caps ignore financing, capex, insurance, taxes, and collection loss. Some Midwest markets are discussed online as higher nominal gross yield markets, while faster-growing Sun Belt metros may trade at lower current yields with different appreciation and expense profiles. Model net cash flow and risk explicitly rather than ranking cities by a headline percentage from a blog table.
