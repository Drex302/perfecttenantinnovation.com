The honest picture in 2026

Mississippi's statewide pandemic rental assistance program, RAMP, has ended, and that money is gone. Help today is smaller, more local, and often one-time.

In Jackson, the strongest anchors are energy assistance through the Hinds County Human Resource Agency, free legal help through Mississippi Center for Legal Services, and local housing support programs. Mississippi evictions move fast, so call early, call more than one place, and get legal help before your court date.

Eviction in Mississippi can move very quickly For unpaid rent, Mississippi landlords can give as little as a few days' written notice before going to court, and hearings can be scheduled soon after. If you get a notice or court papers, call Mississippi Center for Legal Services the same day, and go to your hearing even if you've applied for help.

One-time rent help

211 (United Way)
Start here
The fastest way to find out which rent programs are taking applications right now. Funding at local charities opens and closes during the year, and 211 tracks it. Free, confidential, and open 24/7.
Phone: Dial 211
Stewpot Community Services
If you may lose housing
A Jackson nonprofit with a housing assistance program for people at risk of becoming homeless. Ask 211 for current hours and how to apply.
Phone: Reach through 211
Hinds County Human Resource Agency (HCHRA)
Housing support
Jackson’s community action agency, offering case management and housing support services in addition to energy assistance.
Address: 258 Maddox Rd, Jackson, MS 39212Phone: (601) 923-3950
What to expect from one-time help Charity rent help is usually a partial payment for one month, paid directly to your landlord, and often limited to once a year. It works best when it closes a gap, like getting you through one bad month, rather than covering rent you can't afford going forward. Many programs also ask for a notice from your landlord showing what you owe.

Utility and energy bill help

If you're choosing between rent and the power bill, getting the utility bill covered can be the fastest way to free up money for rent.

Hinds County Human Resource Agency (LIHEAP)
LIHEAP energy aid
The official Low-Income Home Energy Assistance Program (LIHEAP) agency for Hinds County, including Jackson. It helps eligible households pay energy bills. Appointments can fill up fast, so call or apply as early as you can.
Phone: (601) 923-3950Hours: Mon–Fri, 8:00–5:00
Your utility company
Payment plans
Call before your service is cut off and ask about payment plans, extensions, and any hardship programs. Utilities are often more flexible before a shutoff than after.
Phone: The number on your bill

Free legal help with an eviction

A lawyer can make a real difference in an eviction case. They can catch mistakes in the landlord's paperwork, raise repair problems, negotiate more time, or work out a payment plan. If you're facing eviction, call right away.

Mississippi Center for Legal Services
Free eviction defense
Free civil legal help for eligible low-income residents of Hinds, Copiah, Madison, Rankin, Simpson, Warren, and Yazoo counties, including eviction.
Toll-free: 1-800-498-1804Jackson office: (601) 948-6752Address: 414 S State St, Suite 300, Jackson, MS 39201

Housing advocacy
A nonprofit legal organization listed by HUD as a resource for Mississippi renters facing eviction or housing discrimination.

Housing vouchers and affordable housing

Jackson Housing Authority (JHA)
Voucher list closed
Runs public housing and the Housing Choice Voucher (Section 8) program in Jackson. As of 2026, the voucher waiting list is closed. Public housing uses site-based waiting lists that open as units become available, so call to ask which sites are accepting applications.
Address: 2747 Livingston Rd, Jackson, MS 39213Phone: (601) 362-0885
Mississippi Regional Housing Authority VI
Some lists open
Serves Hinds and surrounding counties. Some of its public housing and affordable housing waiting lists opened online in 2026. Ask 211 or the authority which lists are open now.
Ask: Through 211

Vouchers won't solve an emergency this month, but they're the main source of lasting rent help. When a list opens, apply right away, keep your contact information current with the housing authority, and keep any confirmation number.

What can PTI do for you in this situation?

PTI isn’t emergency cash, and it can’t pay a bill that’s due this week. It works once your landlord joins too, which is free for you. Here’s where it fits.

“I’m behind on rent right now, or I got an eviction notice.”

PTI isn’t the right tool yet. Get emergency help first: call 211 and Mississippi Center for Legal Services at 1-800-498-1804, the same day you get court papers. Once things settle, PTI is built to help keep you from ending up here again.

“I got help this month, but I’m worried about next month.”

This is where PTI is designed to fit. If you’re already talking with your landlord about catching up, ask them about PTI. Once they join, eligible rent reporting can help build your payment history, and eligible missions and campaigns can earn rewards.

“I’m waiting on the Section 8 list.”

Use the wait. Invite your current landlord to PTI, and your on-time payments from then on can start building a verified rental record, so you’re a stronger applicant when your name comes up.

“I’m moving and need a fresh start.”

Make PTI part of your new lease. Ask your new landlord about PTI when you sign. From then on, your Stay Grade builds a portable rental reputation that follows you to your next home.

“My landlord won’t make repairs.”

PTI can’t help with a landlord who won’t respond. Report the problem in writing, keep dated photos, and contact your Housing Authority or local legal aid.

How to apply: get ready before you call

Most programs ask for similar documents. Having them ready can save days.

Photo ID for adults in the home
Your lease
Proof of income (pay stubs, benefits letter)
Notice from your landlord showing what you owe
Any eviction notice or court papers
Recent utility bill or shutoff notice
1
Talk to your landlord early
Let your landlord know you're applying for help and ask whether they'll wait or accept a payment plan. Many landlords would rather wait for an assistance check than go to court. Get any agreement in writing.
2
Call several places the same day
Don't wait to hear back from one program before calling the next. Funds are limited, and they often go to whoever applies first.
3
If there's a court case, call a lawyer first
Rent help takes time. A lawyer can help you respond to the court on time while assistance is being processed. Don't skip your court date because you've applied for help.
4
Keep records of everything
Write down who you talked to, when, and what they said. Keep copies of applications and messages with your landlord.
Please call before you go. Funding, hours, and eligibility at local programs change often.
