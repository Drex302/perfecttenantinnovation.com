How to Get Your First Review as a Marketplace Merchant

Zero reviews creates a trust gap no photo alone closes. The fix is not “hope strangers review you”—it is a short sequence of legitimate orders, a great arrival experience, and a well-timed honest ask. Here is a policy-conscious playbook for PTI Shopping Universe and other marketplaces.

The zero-review problem feels circular: buyers hesitate without proof, and proof waits on buyers. The practical unlock is almost always warm, legitimate orders first—people who will actually open the package, use the product, and can speak in specifics.

What usually fails: generic blast emails before delivery, “please rate us five stars” pressure, or anything that reads like compensation for a rating. Those approaches create weak copy for future buyers and real terms-of-service / endorsement risk.

What this guide covers
Who to ask (and in what order). Seven tactics that produce first reviews without sounding desperate. Message templates for check-in → ask. What makes a review persuasive. Insert card structure. Platform notes (high level). How local context on PTI can make early asks feel natural.

Who to ask first: order matters

  1. People who already love your product — past pop-up or farmers market buyers, repeat locals, tasters who sought you out. They can describe a real use case.
  2. Friends or family who will truly use the item — not vague cheerleading. You want specificity (“this replaced my usual rub for pork shoulder”), not obligation stars.
  3. Neighbors and community members who already know your story — especially relevant on hyperlocal channels.
  4. Cold buyers after momentum — once a handful of credible reviews exists, strangers are far more likely to leave organic feedback when you ship well and ask once, politely.

Timing beats clever copy
Asking before the product arrives trains buyers to ignore you. The best window is usually a few days after delivery—after they have opened the box and formed an opinion. A short check-in first, then a review ask if the reply is positive, often gets a better response than one long combined message.

Seven tactics that produce first reviews

1

Seed real experience with a small supporter list

Most reliable

Before you lean on ads, route a small batch of orders to people who will genuinely use the product. After they have had several days with it, ask for an honest review and include a direct link to the review surface your platform provides.

Do not request “five stars.” Do not ask people who never received the goods.

2

Ship a simple insert card in every package

One-time setup

A short thank-you, one sentence on why reviews help a small shop, and a QR code or URL to the listing or review flow. Handwrite one line when volume allows—it reads human.

Avoid “leave five stars for 10% off.” Incentivized ratings are a common policy violation.

3

Two-step message: check-in, then ask

High response

Use marketplace messaging if available. Message 1: confirm arrival and satisfaction. If they reply positively, message 2: short review ask + link. This respects the relationship and reads less like spam.

Do not send three-plus nudges; that damages trust and may violate platform messaging rules.

4

Mine past in-person customers

Warm leads

Text or email people who already bought at a fair or market: you have launched online, here is the listing, if they are willing to leave a quick honest review it helps a new shop. Include the link.

5

Use local framing on PTI Shopping Universe

PTI angle

On PTI, many buyers already chose local on purpose. A humble “you bought local—if the product landed well, a sentence on the listing helps the next neighbor decide” often lands better than a generic corporate template. Features and review timing still depend on current PTI product behavior—confirm in-app or merchant help.

6

Earn the review before you ask

Foundational

Clean packing, accurate description, fast accurate shipping, and a tiny delight (note, sample, care card) lift response rates because buyers want to reciprocate.

7

Reply to every public review quickly

Momentum

A thoughtful seller reply signals you read feedback. Future buyers see an active shop—and future reviewers see their words matter.

Message templates that respect the relationship

Post-delivery check-in (day ~3 after delivery)

Hi [First name],

This is [Your name] from [Shop name]. Your [product] should have arrived—hope it looks and works the way you expected. If anything is off, reply here and I will fix it.

— [Your name]

This is not the review ask. If they answer positively, send the follow-up below.

Follow-up: honest review ask

Glad it worked for you. If you have a minute, an honest review on the listing helps a small shop a lot. Here is the link: [link]

Either way—thank you for supporting what I make. — [Your name]

Short, specific, low pressure. “Either way” keeps the relationship intact.

Warm contact who already tried your product

Hi [Name] — I just put [shop] on [platform]. You have already tried [product], so you know what it is. If you are open to leaving a quick honest review, it would help a new listing a ton: [link]

No pressure either way.

“Already tried” keeps integrity: you are not asking strangers to cosplay customers.

What a helpful review looks like

Reviews that convert strangers mention use case, what surprised them, and who it is for. You can coach taste without scripting: “If you are comfortable, mention how you used it and anything that stood out about shipping or packaging.”

What not to say
Do not ask for a “good” or “five-star” review. Do not tell people what to write. Detailed four- and five-star reviews with pros and a minor con often outperform a wall of identical hype—serious buyers read for credibility.

Insert card wording (print-friendly)

Sample insert

Thank you for your order.

I make [product] in [city]—your support keeps a small shop going. If it landed well and you have a moment, an honest review on [platform] helps the next customer decide.

Scan to leave feedback → [QR → review or listing URL]

Cardstock + a QR code cuts friction. Response rates depend on category and experience—track your own baseline instead of trusting a fixed percentage from the internet.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get my first review on a marketplace when I have no sales?

Start with people who will genuinely use the product: friends, past market customers, or local supporters. Have them purchase (or follow whatever legitimate order flow your platform allows), experience the product for several days, then send a short, honest review ask with a direct link. Avoid pre-shipment asks and avoid incentivized or scripted five-star requests—those create trust and policy risk. Once a small cluster of detailed reviews exists, cold traffic converts more easily.

Is it okay to ask friends and family for reviews?

It is acceptable when they truly bought and used the product and the review reflects their honest experience. It is not acceptable to manufacture reviews for people who did not receive the item, to script specific praise, or to trade discounts for stars. Follow your marketplace’s current terms and the FTC’s endorsement guidance; when in doubt, prioritize disclosure and authenticity over volume.

How many reviews does a new listing need to be competitive?

There is no universal number—category and price point matter. Many operators treat a handful of detailed, credible reviews as a practical trust floor before scaling paid traffic, then build toward a larger set over months. Focus on review quality and specificity (use case, shipping, product detail) rather than chasing identical five-star blurbs.

First reviews are a system—not a lottery.

Ship like you mean it, ask once after value is proven, and keep every review policy-clean. On PTI, lean into local intent without cutting corners.
