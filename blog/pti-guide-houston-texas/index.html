PTI for Houston landlords

Texas’s residential framework is often described as relatively owner-favorable compared with many coastal states: no statewide rent control, and non-payment processes can move quickly when procedures are followed correctly in court. Houston itself does not layer a separate city-wide rent control regime on top of state law for most private residential leases.

PTI’s pitch for owners is operational: predictable software membership, documented communication, and ecosystem connections — instead of automatically defaulting to a percentage-of-rent manager on every door.

Texas landlord-tenant law in Houston (orientation)

Most private residential leases in the city are analyzed under Texas Property Code Chapter 92, but exemptions exist for certain small landlords and property types. Use this list to guide questions for counsel — not as a substitute for it.

  • Deposits: timelines and deduction rules depend on whether Chapter 92 applies to your lease — verify before you promise tenants a specific return date.
  • Repairs: for qualifying conditions, written notice and reasonable cure periods matter — document every step.
  • Non-payment: Texas commonly uses a short notice-to-vacate window for rent non-payment in covered leases; eviction still requires proper court process — not DIY lockouts.
  • Rent control: Texas law limits cities from imposing classic rent caps on most private housing — lease and notice still govern increases at renewal.
  • Source of income / vouchers: federal fair housing and program rules still matter; state and local nuance changes — confirm advertising and screening practices with counsel.

What should Houston renters know about Texas rental law basics?

Most residential leases in Houston fall under Texas Property Code Chapter 92, but exemptions can apply depending on property type and portfolio size. Tenants should understand deposit handling rules that apply to their lease, written repair notice practices for serious conditions, lawful eviction process (not self-help lockouts), and that Texas has no statewide rent control. This is educational orientation only — not legal advice; confirm facts with Texas-licensed counsel.
