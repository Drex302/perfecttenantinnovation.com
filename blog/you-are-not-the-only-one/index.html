If You're Behind on Rent, You're Not Alone — and It's Not Your Fault Alone

Landlords filed 1.23 million eviction cases in a single year across the places researchers track — nearly four every minute. Being behind isn't a personal failure. It's a system built on margins too thin to survive one bad week. Here's how to get on the other side of it.

By Perfect Tenant Innovation  ·  October 1, 2026  ·  For Renters  ·  Sourced from Princeton University's Eviction Lab

The Scale It's the Margin, Not You The Crisis Is Built Built to Get You Out How PTI Helps
Start here, because it matters If you've gotten an eviction notice, or you're lying awake doing the math before the first of the month, the most important thing to know is this: you are one of millions, and the reason has far more to do with how the system is built than with anything you did wrong. That's not an excuse — it's a map. Once you see the real cause, you can see the real way out. This post is about both.

The scale

Princeton's Eviction Lab tracks eviction filings across 10 states and 38 cities — areas that together are home to about a third of America's renters. In a single recent year, landlords in just those tracked places filed 1.23 million eviction cases. Nationally, researchers estimate that works out to nearly four eviction filings every minute, around 6,300 Americans facing eviction every single day.

Sit with that number for a second, because it changes how you should feel about your own situation. Over a million households, in a third of the country, in one year. The notice on your door isn't a sign that you failed where everyone else succeeded. It's the single most common financial crisis in American renting — and the people it happens to are, overwhelmingly, people who were doing everything they were supposed to.

It's the margin, not you

Here's what the data makes clear once you zoom out. Most eviction filings aren't about reckless tenants. They're about math. Across the country, a huge share of renting families spend half or more of their income on rent. When that's the case, there is no cushion. A reduced shift, a car that won't start, a medical bill, a late paycheck — any one of them can turn "current" into "behind" overnight, not because of a bad decision, but because the margin was never there to begin with.

Eviction isn't a character flaw. It's what happens when rent takes so much of a paycheck that one unexpected expense becomes a housing emergency.

That reframe matters because shame keeps people stuck. People who believe eviction is a personal failing hide it, avoid dealing with it, and miss the moments when it could still be solved. People who understand it's a margin problem can do something about the margin — and about the record it threatens to leave.

The crisis is built — which means it can be built differently

If there's one lesson from looking at eviction nationally, it's that none of this is natural or inevitable. The crisis is built — out of specific, changeable things: low filing fees that make it cheap for landlords to file, fast court timelines, thin margins, and screening systems that punish you for a single filing for years. Everything on that list was decided by people. And anything decided by people can be decided differently.

You can't personally rewrite your state's eviction laws before the first of the month. But you can change your own position inside the system — your margin, your credit, your record, your leverage with a landlord. Those are the levers within reach, and they're exactly the ones the right tools are built to move.

Built to get you out of the statistic

The goal isn't to feel better about being one of 1.23 million. It's to stop being one. That comes down to a few concrete things, each of which is within a renter's reach with the right system behind them:

Turn rent into credit. The money you already pay every month can build your credit score instead of vanishing — strengthening every future application and loan.
Handle the tight month before it becomes a filing. A structured payment plan with your landlord keeps a shortfall off your record, where it would otherwise cost you for years.
Build leverage and stability. A documented history of on-time payment is proof you're a reliable tenant — the thing that gets applications approved and gives you standing when you need flexibility.
