PTI for Charlotte landlords

🏠 Landlord guide · Charlotte, NC

North Carolina’s framework under Chapter 42 includes a two-month security deposit cap on many fixed-term leases (held in a trust account), a 10-day non-payment notice when properly executed, and no statewide rent control. Charlotte’s growth and relocation-heavy renter base make turnover costs and documentation especially important — confirm INLIVIAN program rules if you participate voluntarily.

North Carolina landlord-tenant law in Charlotte (educational)

North Carolina General Statutes Chapter 42 governs most residential leases. Charlotte does not add a separate rent-control layer. Themes to research with counsel:

  • Security deposit cap: Up to two months’ rent on many fixed-term leases; trust account required; tenant notice of institution within 30 days.
  • Deposit return: 30 days with itemized deductions when applicable.
  • Non-payment: 10-day notice to quit before Summary Ejectment filing in Mecklenburg when properly executed.
  • Entry: Reasonable notice — 24 hours commonly cited for non-emergency access.
  • No rent control: NC preempts local rent control.
  • Source of income: No statewide protection requiring HCV acceptance — INLIVIAN participation is voluntary; confirm program rules if you opt in.
